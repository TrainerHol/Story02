# Plot Outline: Coiling Garden

Simple structure for YOLO mode - major beats without overwhelming detail.

## Setting

1920s, semi-steampunk. A country at war — distant artillery, rationed gas, zeppelin patrols overhead. The Rilutz estate sits outside a town that's slowly emptying as the conflict creeps closer. Clockwork mechanisms mix with art deco elegance. The hedge maze is overgrown, the mansion half-shuttered. The world runs on steam and brass and things people choose not to explain.

## Tone

Magical realism. The supernatural is never announced — it seeps in through the cracks. The studio underground shouldn't exist but does. The serpent's touch is real but unexplained. Nobody discusses it. The war is present but peripheral — heard in the distance, felt in the shortages, visible in the zeppelins that cross the sky like slow, bloated fish. Slice of life amid collapse.

## Three-Act Structure

### Act 1: Setup
_Roughly the first 25% of your story (~8,750 words)_

**Opening Scene:**
Mu-Poe arrives at the Rilutz estate under a name that isn't hers, filling a position meant for someone who never showed. Wartime — nobody checks. She cleans, she dusts, she studies Mable's sculptures with hands that know more than they should. The estate is beautiful and half-abandoned. Theo Thregee is already there, measuring and cataloging with his insufferable mustache and his leather-bound ledgers. Zeppelins drift overhead. The town is quiet in the way towns get before they empty.

**Inciting Incident:**
Her dog bolts into the hedge maze at night. Mu-Poe chases after it and finds a concealed entrance — steps leading down into earth. In total darkness, she discovers a studio: tools, clay, stone. When she strikes a match, there's nothing but bare ground. The studio only exists when she cannot see it.

**Commitment Point:**
She returns alone the next night, deliberately in the dark. She touches the clay and feels hands close over hers — not threatening, but guiding. The air coils. Something shapes alongside her. She should be terrified. Instead she stays. She goes back the next night. And the next. The daily rhythm of her double life begins — maid by day, sculptor in the dark by night.

---

### Act 2: Confrontation
_The middle 50% - where most of your story lives (~17,500 words)_

**Slice-of-life rhythm — daily textures of the story:**

- Mu-Poe's daytime life at the estate: cleaning, cooking, dodging Theo's questions, watching the war creep closer through small details — fewer deliveries, more soldiers on the road, the baker's son gone. The mundane rituals of maintaining a dead woman's house while the world outside disintegrates.
- Her nighttime life in the studio: learning to sculpt entirely by touch. The serpent's presence grows more distinct — the way the air warms, the way clay moves under invisible hands alongside hers. A dialogue told through form. What starts as guidance becomes collaboration, then intimacy. A romance conducted in total darkness.
- Mu-Poe begins to understand what Mable did — not through journals or documents (there are none), but through the sculptures themselves. The way the serpent's hands move. The impossible precision of Mable's famous works. The feeling that the perfection was never Mable's own. Self-discovery, not revelation.
- Theo grows suspicious — not of the supernatural, but of Mu-Poe. Her references don't exist. The hiring agency has no record of her. He starts asking pointed questions, checking her story, watching her nighttime movements from his upstairs window.
- Small moments of daily life give the story its texture: Mu-Poe fixing a leaking pipe with wire and steam-solder. Sharing tea with a neighbor who's thinking about leaving town. Walking the dog past a requisitioned field. The war as weather — always there, shaping everything, never the point.

**Midpoint Turn:**
Mu-Poe's own sculptures — the ones she makes in the dark with the serpent's hands over hers — are extraordinary. She brings one upstairs. In daylight, it's breathtaking. But she can't tell where her work ends and the serpent's begins. The question that haunts the rest of the story takes shape: is this hers? Is she becoming an artist, or is she becoming another Mable?

---

### Act 3: Resolution
_Final 25% - bringing everything together (~8,750 words)_

**Climax:**
Theo cracks it. He finds proof that Mu-Poe isn't the maid who was hired — wrong name, wrong background, wrong everything. He confronts her, threatens to expose her to the estate's trustees, to have her arrested for fraud. The war has made authorities twitchy about impostors. This isn't just losing a job — in wartime, this is prison. Mu-Poe's double life collapses. The secret of the studio, the serpent, the art — all of it is threatened by the most mundane danger possible: a man with a mustache and a filing discrepancy.

**Resolution:**
Before the exposure can play out — before Theo can send his letters or summon anyone — a zeppelin crashes into the hedge maze. Whether shot down, mechanical failure, or something stranger is left ambiguous. The maze burns. The entrance to the underground studio is buried or transformed. In the aftermath — fire, wreckage, chaos — the old order of the estate is shattered. Theo's ledgers, his proof, his tidy narrative — scattered. The war has arrived at the doorstep, and suddenly nobody cares about a maid's paperwork. What matters is what survives. Mu-Poe emerges from the wreckage carrying something — a sculpture, maybe, or just the knowledge in her hands. The serpent's fate is ambiguous. The art remains.

## Plot Threads to Track

Quick checklist of key storylines to remember:

- [ ] Mu-Poe's artistic growth — from admirer to creator
- [ ] The romance with the serpent — told through touch and sculpture in darkness
- [ ] Mable's legacy — understood through the art itself, not documents
- [ ] Mu-Poe's imposture — she is not who she says she is
- [ ] Theo's investigation — paperwork, suspicion, the insufferable mustache
- [ ] The war — background texture that becomes foreground catastrophe
- [ ] The question of authorship — whose art is it, really?
- [ ] The studio's rules — darkness as condition, metaphor, and intimacy

## Pacing Notes

_Add notes as you draft about what's working_

Slice of life is the engine. The supernatural and the war are both present but peripheral for most of the story — the daily rhythms of cleaning, sculpting, eating, walking the dog, dodging Theo. Let the mundane carry the weight. The zeppelin crash should feel like the war finally breaking through the membrane of daily life — sudden, violent, transformative. Contrast the slow tactile intimacy of the studio scenes against the brisk, tense daytime exchanges with Theo.

## Development Notes

This is a YOLO starter outline - high-level structure without scene-by-scene detail.

**As you write:**
- Scenes will emerge between these beats
- Subplots may develop naturally
- The path from beat to beat can surprise you
- Some beats may shift or merge

**The YOLO approach:** These are guideposts, not prescriptions. Major story beats are mapped, but the journey between them is yours to discover. If the story wants to go somewhere unexpected, follow it. Update this outline to match what you actually write, not the other way around.

**When to update:**
- After writing major turning points
- When a subplot emerges
- If the story takes an unexpected turn
- When you discover the real theme

This outline serves you - don't let it become a constraint.
